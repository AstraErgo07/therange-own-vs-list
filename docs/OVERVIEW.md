# Own vs List (The Range, part of CDS): overview

Version v1.0. Generated by `tools/build_overview.py` from the code, at the default settings, so the numbers match the app. This is the plain-text version of the Streamlit app. Code: https://github.com/AstraErgo07/therange-own-vs-list.

Portfolio project. Made by Ami Sata.

## Purpose

For each product a marketplace partner sells on therange.co.uk (3P), should The Range buy and stock it itself (1P), leave it with the partner, or test first? The tool compares the profit per unit of selling it 1P with the value of the 3P sale (commission less marketplace cost, plus the 1P substitute sales it does not take away), checks the return on the money tied up in stock, and ranks the products. The calculations are plain Python rules. A person accepts or overrides every recommendation. An LLM is optional and only drafts the explanation.

## What is real and what is assumed

| Item | Source |
|---|---|
| Listings | Real: public therange.co.uk listings (prices, who sells them), partner names replaced with labels. |
| Commission | Real, published: 13% to 15% by category. VAT 20%. |
| Units sold | Assumed: from a price curve per category (The Range shows almost no reviews). |
| Costs | Assumed: cost of goods, pick-pack-deliver, returns, markdowns, days of stock, units a week, week-to-week demand swing, share of buyers who would otherwise buy The Range's own item. |
| Policy | Minimum yearly return on stock money 25%, holding cost 20% a year. |
| Where to change them | Sidebar sliders. |

## Summary at the default settings

44 therange.co.uk listings: 25 sold by marketplace partners (the candidates) and 19 sold by The Range (used as the substitute a buyer might otherwise choose). Partner names are replaced with labels. Default settings: Bring in-house 6, Test as 3P first 9, Keep 3P 10.

Decision rules, in order:

1. If weekly demand swings by more than the volatility limit (50% of the average), test as 3P first.
2. If 1P and 3P are within 10% of each other, or the return on stock is within 10% of the hurdle, test as 3P first.
3. If 1P makes more per unit than 3P and the return on stock beats the 25% hurdle, bring it in-house.
4. Otherwise keep it with the partner (3P).

## Worked example: 1P against 3P

Top-ranked product: HT-3P-02, Jasmine Printed Duvet Set (home textiles, sold by Seller A).

| Step | Working | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shelf price (avg selling price per unit) | price on the listing, VAT included | £19.99 | Real (listing or published fee) |
| Units a week | a home textiles item at £15 is assumed to sell 25 a week; dearer items sell fewer (25 x (15 / 19.99)) | 18.8 a week, 975 a year | Assumed (slider) |
| Est. GMV a year | 975 units x £19.99 | £19,500 | Worked out |
| If The Range stocks it (1P): money kept per sale | £19.99 / 1.20 (VAT goes to HMRC) | £16.66 | Real (listing or published fee) |
| less cost of the goods | 40% of £16.66 | -£6.66 | Assumed (slider) |
| less pick, pack and delivery | per order | -£3.00 | Assumed (slider) |
| less refunds and clearance discounts | returns 5% + markdowns 15% of £16.66 | -£3.33 | Assumed (slider) |
| less cost of holding stock | £6.66 x 20% a year x 75 days / 365 | -£0.27 | Assumed (slider) |
| = 1P profit per unit | the lines above | £3.39 | Worked out |
| If the partner keeps selling it (3P): commission | 13% of £19.99 (published rate) - £0.50 cost to serve the sale | £2.10 | Real (listing or published fee) and assumed (slider) |
| less sales taken from The Range's own range | The Range's own similar item (median price £7.49) makes no profit (-£0.61 a unit), so the partner's sales cost The Range nothing | £0.00 | Assumed (slider) |
| = 3P value per unit | commission less the lost own sales | £2.10 | Worked out |
| 1P advantage a year | (£3.39 - £2.10) x 975 units | £1,259 | Worked out |
| Return on the money tied up in stock | 1P profit a year £3,306 / average stock value £1,339 (75 days of stock at cost) | 247% (needs at least 25%) | Policy (slider) |
| How much demand swings week to week | assumed 35% of the average; above 50% means test as 3P first | 35% | Assumed (slider) |
| Decision | 1P makes more per unit and the return on stock clears 25% | Bring in-house | Worked out |

## How sensitive is the answer?

| Scenario | Bring in-house | Test as 3P first | Keep 3P | Flipped vs default |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diversion 10% | 6 | 9 | 10 | 0 |
| Diversion 30% | 6 | 9 | 10 | 0 |
| Diversion 50% | 6 | 9 | 10 | 0 |
| COGS estimate -10% pts | 16 | 6 | 3 | 12 |
| COGS estimate +10% pts | 0 | 4 | 21 | 11 |

Diversion is the share of partner buyers who would otherwise buy The Range's own item; COGS is the cost of goods. Changing diversion flips few products; moving the cost-price estimate by 10 points flips many. Supplier cost is the number to get first.

Note: where the price curve is set to 1 (home textiles, outdoor, beauty), units fall in exact proportion as price rises, so every item in that category gets the same GMV a year, for example £19,500 for home textiles. GMV is therefore not a useful ranking here; the ranking uses profit per unit and return on stock instead.

## Limits

- A small sample of listings: a test of the method, not market sizing.
- Units come from a price curve, and the cost benchmarks are judgement, not CDS data. Every £ figure scales with them; supplier cost is the number to get first.
- The Range's pages were read with an automated page-reading tool, one at a time, no logins, robots.txt respected. What The Range's terms say about reuse of content is quoted on the data and method page.
